IL-1 receptor marks the inflammatory endothelial population in psoriasis but is depleted in lesional skin

BACKGROUND: Psoriasis transcriptomic studies are usually single-cohort and rarely separate tissue composition from cell state, leaving the signature's cellular origin unresolved. STAT3 is widely proposed as a therapeutic node, while IL-1 blockade has disappointed in plaque disease for unclear reasons.

OBJECTIVES: To define a reproducible whole-skin psoriasis signature and localise the disease-associated program to specific cells.

METHODS: Of 35 recount3 psoriasis studies screened, four treatment-naive whole-skin cohorts qualified (141 healthy, 145 lesional). Differential expression (limma-voom) was pooled by random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian-Laird), with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR throughout. In 89,058 single cells across healthy, peri-lesional and lesional skin, deconvolution quantified cell-type proportions and Scissor identified cells co-varying with disease; endothelium was stratified by IL1R1.

RESULTS: 2,277 genes were differentially expressed reproducibly across cohorts (FDR<0.05, |log2FC|>1). STAT3 was elevated in all four (+1.06, FDR=5.7x10-6) but heterogeneous (I2=92%) and not significant per-cell (q=0.13), indicating tissue-level rather than cell-intrinsic elevation. Endothelium was the most disease-associated of ten lineages (OR=5.24) and rose in proportion from healthy to lesional skin (q=1.3x10-19). Within endothelium, IL1R1+ cells were enriched 2.3-fold among disease-associated cells (OR=2.32, p=1.4x10-38), expressing far more STAT3 (66% vs 40%), IL6 (31% vs 13%), GSDMD and PYCARD. Yet IL1R1+ cells fell as a fraction of endothelium from healthy to lesional skin (51%, 29%, 27%), while the antagonist IL1RN rose.

SIGNIFICANCE: The vasculature, not STAT3 broadly, carries the disease-associated program, concentrated in an IL-1-responsive subset that is progressively lost. This may explain the limited efficacy of IL-1 blockade in established plaques and directs attention to early vascular change.
