The IL-1 superfamily signal in psoriasis is IL-36, not IL-1b, and its receptor marks a shrinking inflammatory endothelial subset

BACKGROUND: Psoriasis carries increased cardiovascular risk and psoriatic endothelium adopts a pro-atherogenic phenotype, yet transcriptomic studies are usually single-cohort and rarely separate tissue composition from cell state. IL-1 blockade has disappointed in plaque disease, and STAT3 is a widely proposed but untested therapeutic node.

OBJECTIVES: To define a reproducible whole-skin psoriasis signature and localise the disease-associated program to specific cells.

METHODS: Of 35 recount3 psoriasis studies screened, four treatment-naive cohorts qualified (141 healthy, 145 lesional). Differential expression (limma-voom) was pooled by random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian-Laird), Benjamini-Hochberg FDR throughout. In 89,058 single cells across healthy, peri-lesional and lesional skin, deconvolution quantified cell-type proportions and Scissor identified disease-co-varying cells.

RESULTS: 2,277 genes were reproducibly differentially expressed. Within the IL-1 superfamily the signal was carried by IL-36, not IL-1b: IL36A +9.03 (FDR 2x10-19), IL36G +4.66, IL36RN +2.79, with inflammasome effectors CASP5, AIM2, PYCARD, CASP1 and GSDMD all elevated, whereas IL1B (+1.09, I2=94%), IL18 and IL1R1 were unchanged. STAT3, downstream of IL-1-induced IL-6 rather than an IL-1 family member, was elevated (+1.06, FDR 6x10-6) but not significant per-cell (q=0.13). Endothelium was the most disease-associated of ten lineages (OR=5.24), rising in proportion across tiers (q=1x10-19); within it, IL1R1+ cells were enriched 2.3-fold among disease-associated cells (OR=2.32) and expressed far more STAT3 (66% vs 40%) and IL6 (31% vs 13%), yet fell from 51% to 27% of endothelium.

SIGNIFICANCE: IL-36 carries the tissue IL-1-family signal while IL-1 responsiveness concentrates in a contracting endothelial subset, offering a rationale for IL-36-directed rather than IL-1b-directed therapy.
